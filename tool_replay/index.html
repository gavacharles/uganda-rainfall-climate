    Key moments

    About this storm

    Rain fell on Kampala on Saturday 10 October and again from about 2 a.m. on Sunday 11 October. News reports placed flooding at Lubigi, where the Kampala–Hoima road went under water, at Kinawataka, Banda and Kimbejja. Two people died when a wall collapsed onto a rented room.

    The replay shows water rising along the valley channels in the small hours of Sunday, peaking around 3–5 a.m., and draining within hours. Radar satellite passes on Sunday evening and Monday at dawn found almost no standing water.

    Satellite rain places Saturday's first storm cell in the late morning; reports describe an evening downpour at Kimbejja and Lubigi. Satellite rain at 4 km cannot pin down a single storm's timing.

    How it works

    Hourly satellite rain (PERSIANN-CCS, about 4 km) falls on each of Kampala's 6,914 channel stretches. The flow each stretch receives comes from the rational method: rain intensity over the time the catchment takes to respond, its paved share and area. Flow beyond what the channel can carry spills onto the valley floor; the water level comes from the shape of the ground beside each stretch, and depth is that level minus the height of each 30 m square above its drain.

    The model peaks at Lubigi and Kinawataka between 3 and 5 a.m. on 11 October, when the Hoima road was reported under water. It misses Banda, where flooding was probably surface water on streets, which this model does not include. ×1.5 sits between the satellite rain as measured and the 1.9-fold gauge correction found for Kampala's heaviest days.

    Limits

    Ground heights are 30 m satellite terrain (Copernicus GLO-30), accurate to a few metres: the depths show the pattern of the flood, not the depth at any one door. Street drains, single blocked culverts, walls, surface water away from channels and Lake Victoria's level are not modelled. No satellite flood map exists for this storm: the water drained before the radar passed.
