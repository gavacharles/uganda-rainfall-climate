    What happened. Rain fell on Kampala late on the morning of Saturday 10 October and again from about 2 a.m. on Sunday 11 October. News reports placed flooding at Lubigi, where the Kampala–Hoima road went under water, at Kinawataka, Banda and Kimbejja. Two people died when a wall collapsed onto a rented room.

    What the replay shows. Water rises along the valley channels in the small hours of Sunday, peaks around 3–5 a.m., and drains within hours. Radar satellite passes 13 and 25 hours later found almost no standing water.

    Water depth

    How it works. Hourly satellite rain (PERSIANN-CCS, about 4 km) falls on each of Kampala's 6,914 channel stretches. The flow each stretch receives comes from the rational method (rain intensity over the time the catchment takes to respond, its paved share and area). Flow beyond what the channel can carry spills onto the valley floor; the water level comes from the shape of the ground beside each stretch, and depth is that level minus the height of each 30 m square above its drain. This is the Kampala drainage screening model, run hour by hour.

    The model peaks at Lubigi and Kinawataka between 3 and 5 a.m. on 11 October, when the Hoima road was reported under water. It misses Banda, which has little valley floor near a mapped channel: flooding there is probably surface water on streets, which this model does not include.

    Why ×1.5 by default. Satellite rain at 4 km spreads a thunderstorm's core over a large area. Against Kampala's rain gauge, satellite rain underestimated the heaviest days about 1.9-fold. ×1.5 sits between the satellite as measured and that gauge correction; use the slider to see both ends.

    Limits. Ground heights are 30 m satellite terrain (Copernicus GLO-30), accurate to a few metres; the depths show the pattern of the flood, not the depth at any one door. Street drains, culvert blockages at single crossings, walls and surface water away from channels are not modelled. Lake Victoria's level is not modelled. No satellite flood map exists for this storm: the floodwater drained before the radar passed.
