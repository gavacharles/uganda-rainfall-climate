    Key moments

    About this storm

    News reports placed flooding at Lubigi, where the Kampala–Hoima road went under water, at Kinawataka, Banda and Kimbejja. Two people died when a wall collapsed onto a rented room. Radar satellite passes on Sunday evening and Monday at dawn found almost no standing water: the floods rose and drained within hours.

    Satellite rain places Saturday's first storm cell in the late morning; reports describe an evening downpour at Kimbejja and Lubigi. Rain measured from space at 4 km cannot pin down a single storm's timing.

    Light

    With Time of the replay, the sun rises and sets over Kampala as the storm plays: night falls at about 18:40 and day breaks at about 06:30.

    How it works

    Hourly satellite rain (PERSIANN-CCS, about 4 km) falls on each of Kampala's 6,914 channel stretches. The flow each stretch receives comes from the rational method: rain intensity over the time the catchment takes to respond, its paved share and area. Flow beyond what the channel can carry spills onto the valley floor; the water level comes from the shape of the ground beside each stretch, and depth is that level minus the height of each 30 m square above its drain. This is the Kampala drainage screening model, run hour by hour.

    Why no lagoon or tide setting, as in some flood maps: Kampala's channels run to Lake Victoria through long valleys, and the model does not include the lake backing up. Silt and paving are the levers that matter here.

    Limits

    Ground heights are 30 m satellite terrain (Copernicus GLO-30), accurate to a few metres: the depths show the pattern of the flood, not the depth at any one door. Street drains, single blocked culverts, walls, surface water away from channels and Lake Victoria's level are not modelled. No satellite flood map exists for this storm: the water drained before the radar passed.
