## 1. The bay, scene by scene [269]
Attach `animations/ka04_murchison_bay_scenes.gif`

```
Inner Murchison Bay, where Kampala gets its drinking water, in 13 satellite views since 2018.

One colour scale in every frame: dark is clear water, yellow is muddy.

The mud is usually thickest in the north of the bay, near Port Bell, where Kampala's channels pour in.
```

**Alt text:** Animation of 13 Sentinel-2 views of Inner Murchison Bay, 2018 to 2026, with satellite turbidity coloured from dark purple (clear) to yellow (muddy) on one fixed scale. The muddiest water is usually in the northern bay near Port Bell.

## 2. Why it matters (reply) [263]

```
Why it matters: the Ggaba waterworks draw Kampala's water from this bay.

The wetlands that once filtered the city's runoff on its way here have halved since 1986 (1,200 → 587 ha), and the water at the channel outlets has grown muddier relative to the open lake.
```

---

**Sources.** Sentinel-2 turbidity (Dogliotti et al. 2015), 13 views with at least a quarter of the bay cloud-free; wetland and outlet figures from paper P2, *The double loss*. Turbidity from space is a relative index, not a lab measurement.
