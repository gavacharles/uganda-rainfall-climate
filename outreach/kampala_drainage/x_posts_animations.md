## 2. Water rising through the valleys [229]

**GIF:** `kd3d_water_rising.gif`

Where does the water go when Kampala's drains overflow?

Raise it 3 m above the drains and it reaches 15,000 of the 99,000 buildings in central Kampala's valleys.

The low ground floods first. That's where homes keep being built.

## 7. Radar spots new construction [221]

**GIF:** `kd_a4_radar_new_construction.gif`

Free satellite radar, every year since 2017, flags open land turning into buildings across Kampala.

It misses many small homes, but it catches new estates and factories, including on the valley floors where floods go. 🛰️

## 8. Paving creeps up the catchments [200]

**GIF:** `kd_a5_paving_upstream.gif`

Every roof and road sends rain straight into a drain.

In 1975, 51 km of Kampala's channels drained catchments more than 30% paved. By 2020: 236 km.

The drains stayed the same size. The water didn't.

## 9. What sets flood hotspots apart [211]

**GIF:** `kd_a6_hotspot_drivers.gif`

What do Kampala's flood hotspots have in common?

Not heavier rain. Homes on the floodplain, buildings in the channel reserve, culverts that choke, and wetland that's gone.

Here are all eight pressures, mapped.

## 10. Valley floors, year by year [211]

**GIF:** `kd_a7_valley_landcover.gif`

Kampala's valley floors, 2017 → 2023, from 10 m satellite land cover.

Built-up land went from 30% to 36% in six years. Wetland is down to under 1%.

The places water collects are the places being built fastest.

## 11. How much work is waiting [197]

**GIF:** `kd_a8_priority_vs_emerging.gif`

How much of Kampala's drainage needs work?

138 km of channel is high priority and still getting worse. Another 100 km isn't critical yet but is heading there fast.

That's the to-do list for KCCA.
