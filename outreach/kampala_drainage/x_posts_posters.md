## 1. Kampala spreads across its hills [254]
Attach `animations/ka01_kampala_spreads.gif`

```
Kampala from space, 1986 → 2025.

Inside KCCA, built-up land grew from 40 km² to 56 km². Purple is roofs and roads; green is what's left of the wetland valleys.

The city is now nearly full: only 0.6 km² was added in 2015–2025. Growth has moved outward.
```

**Alt text:** Landsat false-colour time-lapse of Kampala, 1986 to 2025. Purple built-up land spreads over the hills while green wetland valleys narrow. A banner gives built-up area inside KCCA: 40 km² in 1985 rising to 56 km² in 2025.

## 2. Hills built over, valleys filling [278]
Attach `kp01_kampala_false_colour.png`

```
Kampala in false colour.

Purple: the hills, built over.
Green: the wetland valleys, now thin ribbons.

Those valleys used to hold Kampala's storm water and clean what flowed to Lake Victoria. Between 1986 and 2025, those draining to Murchison Bay lost half their green wetland.
```

**Alt text:** Sentinel-2 false-colour image of Greater Kampala, dry seasons 2024–26. Built-up hills appear purple; the remaining wetland valleys appear as thin green ribbons running to Lake Victoria, shown in black.

## 4. Same rain, two cities [253]
Attach `kp03_kampala_roads_glow.png`

```
Same rain, two cities.

An ordinary storm, the kind Kampala gets every two years:
• channels silted, as they are now: 198 km of road under water
• channels clean: 5 km

The cheapest flood defence in Kampala is a shovel, used before every rainy season.
```

**Alt text:** Two dark maps of Kampala's road network. Left, with silted channels as observed: 198 km of road glow pink, under at least 0.3 m of water in an ordinary 2-year storm. Right, with clean channels: 5 km.

## 5. Roads going under as the storm grows [252]

Kampala's roads as the storm grows.

2-year storm: 198 km of road under water.

## 7. Murchison Bay: where the water is muddiest [260]
Attach `kp04_murchison_bay_plume.png`

```
Inner Murchison Bay, where Kampala gets its drinking water.

The muddiest water sits where the city's channels pour in.

As the wetlands that filtered them shrank, outlet water got muddier relative to the open lake: 1.14 → 1.51 times over 40 years of Landsat.
```

**Alt text:** Sentinel-2 image of Inner Murchison Bay on 10 December 2020, with satellite turbidity coloured purple to yellow on the water. The muddiest water sits at the channel outlets on the city shore.
