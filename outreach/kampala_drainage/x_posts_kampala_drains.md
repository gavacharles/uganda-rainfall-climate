## 1. Floods on ordinary rain

**Graphic:** `x1_floods_ordinary_rain.png`

Kampala's storms haven't got heavier: the wettest day of the year shows no rise since 1981.

Yet it keeps flooding. On 6 Mar and 17 Sep 2026, the rain ranked around the 20th percentile of wet days.

It's not just the rain. It's the drains.

## 2. What hotspots have in common

**Graphic:** `kd04_priority_overview.png`

I mapped all 6,914 drainage stretches in Greater Kampala.

The ones that flood stand out for:
• homes on the floodplain
• buildings in the 15 m channel reserve
• culverts and bridges
• wetland almost gone

These are planning problems, not weather.

## 3. We can see the next hotspots coming

**Graphic:** `x2_next_hotspots.png`

We can see Kampala's next flood hotspots coming.

Today it points to the northern and eastern fringe. Act now.

## 5. The vanished wetlands

**Animation:** `kd_a3_wetlands_timelapse.gif`

Kampala's wetlands from space, 1986 → 2025.

The dark green valley floors were papyrus swamps that soaked up storm water. Watch them fill with roofs.

The drains (blue) now carry what the swamps once held.

## 6. People in the water's path

**Graphic:** `x3_people_low_ground.png`

205,000 people now live on low ground within 150 m of a Kampala drainage channel.

In 1975 it was 36,000.

Flood risk isn't only about how much it rains. It's about who lives where the water goes.

## 7. Radar: watching Kampala build (bonus post)

**Main visual:** `kd22_radar_cluster_zooms.png` (radar before/after next to today's imagery). Alternative: the animation `kd_a4_radar_new_construction.gif`, or the city-wide map `kd17_radar_new_construction.png`.

Satellite radar sees Kampala through the clouds.

I compared 10 years of Sentinel-1 passes to flag ground that went from open land to buildings. 55 ha of those leads sit on valley floors, right in the path of floodwater.

New estates show up clearly. 🛰️

## 8. Radar view of Kampala after the 11 October storm

**Graphic:** `kd10_sentinel1_after_storm.png`

Sunday's storm flooded Lubigi, Kinawataka and Banda before dawn.

13 hours later a radar satellite passed over Kampala. Almost no standing water was left.

Kampala's floods rise and drain within hours. The city needs rain gauges and drain sensors, not just satellites. 🛰️

---

Kampala by radar, 13 hours after Sunday's storm.

To track floods, Kampala needs gauges and sensors on its drains, and reports from the people who live by them.

## 10. Bwaise: why it floods on ordinary rain

**Video:** `kd_fly_01_bwaise_kalerwe_social.mp4`

Bwaise floods on rain that barely registers elsewhere.

## 11. Lubigi: the wetland that held the water

**Video:** `kd_fly_06_lubigi_wetland_social.mp4` (vertical, 22 s)

Lubigi was once a papyrus wetland that soaked up Kampala's storm water.

Fly along the channel today: 139 buildings within 15 m of it and 16 dump sites nearby.

When the wetland goes, the water goes into homes.

## 12. Lubigi: Sunday's flood

**Video:** `kd_fly_06_lubigi_wetland_social.mp4`

On Sunday the Kampala–Hoima road flooded at Lubigi.

The fix is not more rain forecasts. It's space for the water.
