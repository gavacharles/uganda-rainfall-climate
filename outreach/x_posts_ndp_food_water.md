## Seasons and NDP IV (attach `g3_season_swap.png`)

```
Uganda plans around the March–May rains. Satellites say that's now the riskier season.

Crop drought in Mar–Jul doubled (11% → 23% of districts) since the 1980s–90s. In Sep–Dec it fell to 3%.

NDP IV's agro-industrialisation drive should plant by the data, not by habit.
```

## Irrigation (attach `g8_irrigation_need.png`)

```
NDP IV plans to double Uganda's irrigated land: 0.77% → 1.54% of irrigable area by 2029/30.

Size it right. At peak, a hectare needs 0.6 l/s in Ankole–Kigezi but 1.3 l/s in Karamoja.

One national design number will overbuild some schemes and starve others.
```

## Urban drainage (attach `g6_kampala_rain_vs_paving.png`)

```
NDP IV admits Kampala's drainage upgrades are "not adequate to address the rainwater runoff".

The rain hasn't grown. The concrete has.

Fix: protect valley floors, store 26–116 m³ of runoff per hectare on site, then add 9–13% to design storms for climate.
```

## Data (attach `g11_design_numbers.png`)

```
Every road, drain, dam and irrigation scheme in NDP IV is designed on rainfall statistics.

Uganda's public daily rain records end in 1986.

Opening the national weather archive may be the cheapest climate-adaptation project in the whole plan.
```

## Water scarcity (attach `g9_water_scarce_map.png`)

```
Where is Uganda short of water?

Karamoja: semi-arid, up to 8 dry months a year.
Acholi–Lango–Teso and the Ankole rangelands: 4–5.

NDP IV wants water-for-production storage to grow from 52.6 to 280.4 million m³ by 2029/30. Start where the map is darkest.
```

## Food security (attach `g10_karamoja_hunger_vs_rain.png`)

```
Karamoja's food crisis didn't simply follow the rain.

Areas in IPC crisis rose from 0% (2011–13) to ~35% (2021–24), and the rise began in the good seasons of 2019–20.

Valley tanks and seed matter, but so do livestock, security and prices.
```

---

## Hot nights (attach `a4_hot_nights.gif`)

```
Uganda's nights are heating up.

Unusually warm nights: about 22 a year in 1981–95, 60 a year in 2011–25, and close to 90 in the last few years.

Hotter nights mean less relief from the day's heat for people, livestock and crops.
```
