## Nairobi (attach `g7_nairobi_card.png`; reply with `a3_nairobi_growth.gif`)

```
Nairobi floods are often blamed on climate change.

The data: Nairobi's 10-year storm hasn't grown since the 1980s (156 → 143 mm).

Its paved surface has (34% → 46%).

Paving since 1975 has raised flood peaks ~31%. Climate change adds 11–17% by the 2050s.
```

## Lagos (attach `g7_lagos_card.png`; reply with `a3_lagos_growth.gif`)

```
Lagos floods every rainy season. Is the rain getting heavier?

Not in 45 years of satellite data: the 10-year storm is flat (133 → 125 mm).

What changed is the ground: paved surface 47% → 62%.

Paving alone has raised flood peaks ~36% since 1975, ~3x the 2050s climate effect.
```

## Dar es Salaam (attach `g7_dar_es_salaam_card.png`; reply with `a3_dar_es_salaam_growth.gif`)

```
Dar es Salaam is the exception: its heavy storms really are getting heavier.

10-year storm: up ~25% since the 1980s.
Paved surface: 26% → 42%.

So Dar faces both. Paving has still raised flood peaks ~27% since 1975, about 2-3x what climate adds by the 2050s.
```

## Johannesburg (attach `g7_johannesburg_card.png`; reply with `a3_johannesburg_growth.gif`)

```
Johannesburg: frequent storms are getting heavier (+16% since the 1980s) AND the city is paving over (38% → 49%).

Rain and concrete now push flood peaks up about equally.

But paving since 1975 (+37%) still outweighs the climate change expected by the 2050s (+18–26%).
```
