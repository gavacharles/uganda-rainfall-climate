## Post 1: rain is more erratic (fig 06)

**Graphic:** `g12_erratic_months.png`

Uganda's rain has become more erratic.

Months with rain 40%+ above or below normal:

1981–95: 8
1996–2010: 20
2011–25: 35

That's 4x more wild months: more floods and more dry spells. Farmers, dam operators and drain designers all need a wider safety margin.

## Post 2: second rains last longer (fig 08, static)

**Graphic:** `g13_second_rains_longer.png`

Uganda's second rains now last 103 days on average, up from 91 in 1981–95.

They end about a week later: early December, not late November.

The first rains have barely moved (68 → 73 days).

Planting calendars drawn up in the 1980s need updating.

## Post 4: East Africa's short rains (fig 21)

**Graphic:** `g14_east_africa_rains.png`

East Africa's October–December rains are getting wetter, about +3.6% per decade since 1981. Eastern Uganda is in the hotspot.

Meanwhile the March–May rains are drying in Somalia and eastern Ethiopia.

Scientists call it the "East African paradox".

---

## Post 5: Uganda warming faster than the world (fig 22)

**Graphic:** `g15_warming_faster.png`

Uganda is warming faster than the world.

Since 1981: +0.51 °C per decade, against +0.37 °C for global land.

2011–25 was 1.5 °C hotter than 1981–95.

That means thirstier crops, faster-evaporating reservoirs and higher water demand in every town.

## Post 6: the warming race (fig 22, animated)

**Animation:** `a6_warming.gif`

45 years of Uganda vs the world, one year at a time.

In the 1980s Uganda ran cooler than its 1991–2020 normal. By 2024 it was 1.5 °C above it, while global land was at 1.1 °C.

Uganda is warming about 40% faster than the world's land.
